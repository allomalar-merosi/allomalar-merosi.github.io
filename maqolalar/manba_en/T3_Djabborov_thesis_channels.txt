ABSTRACT: The sale of 73.71 per cent of Ipoteka Bank to Hungary's OTP Group in 2023 was the first privatization of a state bank to a foreign strategic investor in Uzbekistan. Drawing on agency, corporate governance and international banking theories, this paper classifies the influence of a foreign strategic investor on bank stability into five two-directional channels – capital, corporate governance, risk management, technology and market (funding base) – and links each to measurable indicators. Evidence from the bank's audited IFRS statements for 2019–2025 and a comparison with nine state-owned banks show a sharp negative short-term effect through the recognition of impaired loans and positive medium-term effects through the capital, efficiency and funding channels.

# 1. Introduction

In 2020, 13 of Uzbekistan's 31 banks were state-owned and held about 85 per cent of banking assets; the 2020–2025 reform strategy aimed to attract at least three foreign strategic investors (Presidential Decree No. PF-5992, 2020; GRATA International, 2020). Ipoteka Bank was sold to OTP Group in June 2023 (Ipoteka-bank, 2023). According to its audited IFRS statements, the bank lost UZS 1,616 billion in 2023 after loan-loss provisions of UZS 4,275 billion, then earned UZS 1,287 billion in 2024 and UZS 2,047 billion in 2025, with an ROE of 28.1 per cent (Ipoteka Bank, 2020–2026). Explaining such dynamics requires a systematic view of how a foreign owner affects stability. The literature documents both efficiency gains from foreign ownership in transition economies (Bonin et al., 2005) and contagion through multinational banks in global crises (de Haas & van Lelyveld, 2014), but the mechanisms are usually treated one-sidedly.

# 2. Method

Mechanisms identified in agency theory (Jensen & Meckling, 1976), studies of state bank ownership (La Porta et al., 2002) and research on acquired banks (Havrylchyk & Jurzyk, 2011) were grouped into channels, each with positive and negative mechanisms and observable indicators. The indicators were computed from the bank's audited IFRS statements for 2019–2022 (before the sale) and 2023–2025 (after), and the national-standard indicators were compared with those of the nine banks that remained state-owned, using Central Bank bank-level statistics (CBU, 2026) in a difference-in-differences design. A preliminary expert survey rated short-term (1–2 years) and long-term (3–5 years) effects on a 1–5 scale.

Average Stage 3 loans rose from 5.9 to 19.7 per cent of the portfolio after the acquisition – the clearest negative short-term effect, reflecting the recognition of legacy problems under group standards rather than new lending. The capital channel worked in the positive direction: equity to assets fell to 11.2 per cent after the 2023 loss but recovered to 15.1 per cent by 2025, and the difference-in-differences estimate for the national-standard equity-to-assets ratio relative to state banks is +3.6 percentage points (significant at 10 per cent; Ipoteka ranks first among ten banks in a placebo test). Dependence on state funding fell from 52.4 to 43.0 per cent of liabilities on average, and the share of retail loans rose from 40.2 to 61.6 per cent. The cost-to-income ratio rose from 38.5 to 40.9 per cent as integration costs grew, and the bank's market share declined, so the efficiency gains expected from the governance and technology channels have not yet appeared. In the preliminary expert survey, the short-term effect of the risk-management channel was rated below neutral (<<2.8>>), while all channels were rated positive in the long term, with corporate governance (<<4.6>>) and risk management (<<4.5>>) highest and the market channel lowest (<<3.9>>).

# 4. Conclusion

The influence of a foreign strategic investor on bank stability is two-directional and time-dependent: the risk-management channel produces a sharp negative effect in the integration year, while the capital and funding channels improve stability within two years. Supervisors should monitor indicators for each channel, anticipate temporary deterioration from asset revaluation, restrict dividends while impaired loans remain elevated and coordinate with home supervisors. Future privatizations should include an independent asset quality review before the sale and investor commitments on capital and on the continuation of mortgage and small-business lending.
