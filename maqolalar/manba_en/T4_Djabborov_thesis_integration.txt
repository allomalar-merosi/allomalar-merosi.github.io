ABSTRACT: Bank privatization is often treated as a one-off transaction, while the integration that follows determines whether stability improves. Based on the sale of Ipoteka Bank to OTP Group (2023) and experience from Central and Eastern Europe, this paper proposes a seven-stage integration model – from pre-deal preparation to full ownership – with tasks and indicators for each stage. According to the bank's audited IFRS statements, its return on assets fell from 2.2 per cent in 2022 to −3.6 per cent in the integration year and rose to 2.7 and 3.9 per cent in 2024–2025, a J-shaped path consistent with the model. The price-to-book ratio of about 0.83 implies a discount that proved justified: the loan-loss provisions recognized in 2023 alone exceeded the deal price, underlining the value of asset quality reviews before sale.

# 1. Introduction

Uzbekistan's 2020–2025 banking reform strategy envisaged the privatization of six state banks (Kun.uz, 2020), but only Ipoteka Bank has been sold, and deadlines for others have been postponed repeatedly (Daryo, 2024). Experience from the first deal is therefore valuable. Central and Eastern European privatizations show that sales to strategic investors raise efficiency (Bonin et al., 2005; Megginson, 2005), but acquired banks' profitability often declines before it improves (Havrylchyk & Jurzyk, 2011).

# 2. Method

Public information on the Ipoteka Bank transaction and integration, together with the bank's audited IFRS statements for 2019–2025 and the interim statements for the first half of 2026 (Ipoteka Bank, 2020–2026), was organized chronologically and compared with international experience to derive integration stages. The deal price was assessed by the price-to-book ratio, P/BV = (P / s) / BV, where P is the price, s the stake sold and BV book equity.

# 3. Results

FIG 1: Seven-stage post-acquisition integration model ## Author's elaboration.
1. Pre-deal preparation: asset quality review || deal terms and investor commitments
2. Transfer of governance: new supervisory board and management || delegation matrix
3. Capital and standards alignment: IFRS 9 and group policies || capital injection
4. Asset quality revaluation: recognition of problem loans || provisioning
5. Operational integration: IT systems || products and brand || staff
6. Growth and optimization: business model change || funding diversification
7. Full ownership: purchase of remaining shares || long-term strategy

In Ipoteka Bank, an agreement was announced in December 2022 and the 73.71 per cent stake was transferred in June 2023 (Ipoteka-bank, 2023). In the same year the portfolio was revalued under group standards: loan-loss provisions of UZS 4,275 billion were recognized, credit-impaired (Stage 3) loans rose from 7.0 to 18.7 per cent of gross loans and the bank lost UZS 1,616 billion; OTP injected USD 70 million of capital in December 2023 (Kun.uz, 2023). The bank was rebranded, its loan book shifted towards households (retail loans rose from 45.6 per cent of the portfolio in 2022 to 68.8 per cent in 2025) and dependence on state funding declined from 50.2 to 41.5 per cent of liabilities. Net profit reached UZS 1,287 billion in 2024 and UZS 2,047 billion in 2025, and UZS 1,351 billion in the first half of 2026 (Ipoteka Bank, 2020–2026). In May 2026 the IFC converted a loan into an 8.69 per cent stake, leaving OTP with 72.89 per cent and the Ministry of Economy and Finance with 17.49 per cent, so the seventh stage – full ownership – has not yet been reached. Stages overlapped in practice, but the sequence matches the model, and the stage-four revaluation produced the trough in the bank's ROA (Figure 2).

Using the press-reported price of USD 324 million (bne IntelliNews, 2023), the implied value of 100 per cent of the bank is about USD 440 million. Book equity under national standards was UZS 5.9 trillion at end-2022 (CBU, 2026), or about USD 527 million at the Central Bank's official year-end rate of UZS 11,225 per USD, giving a P/BV of about 0.83; on an IFRS basis (UZS 5.7 trillion, or USD 505 million) the ratio is 0.87. A sale below book value is consistent with the investor pricing in hidden asset-quality problems, and the subsequent revaluation confirmed this: the provisions recognized in 2023 alone (about USD 350 million) exceeded the price paid.

# 4. Conclusion

Integration is a multi-stage process whose fourth stage – asset quality revaluation – predictably worsens reported indicators. Governments should conduct independent asset quality reviews and separate problem assets before sale, define the schedule for withdrawing state funding and investor commitments in the deal, and monitor integration with stage-specific targets rather than judging success by first-year results.
